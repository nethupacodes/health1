      Bedtime routine ideas

      • Pick a regular bedtime and wake-up time, even at weekends if you can.
      • Put screens away about 30 to 60 minutes before bed.
      • Dim the lights, have a wash and get your school bag ready for tomorrow.
      • Try the breathing exercise in MindCare, or read a few pages of a book.
      • Keep your room cool, quiet and dark. Avoid tea, coffee and fizzy drinks late in the day.

      If you often feel unable to sleep or very tired, talk to a trusted adult or a doctor.

        Daily habit checklist

        A balanced plate

        A simple guide: fill about half your plate with vegetables, leaves and fruit, about a quarter with rice or another staple, and about a quarter with a protein food. Drink water with your meal.

        Vegetables and leavesRice, roti or rootsProtein

        This is a rough guide for enjoying meals, not a diet. Every body is different. Ask a doctor or dietitian about your own needs.

        Meal ideas

        Breakfast
        • Pol roti with a boiled egg and a banana
        • String hoppers with dhal curry and a vegetable sambol
        • Kiribath with a piece of fruit, such as papaya
        School lunch
        • Lunch packet: rice, dhal, a green mallum, and egg or fish
        • Vegetable roti with a boiled egg and cucumber slices
        • Boiled chickpeas (kadala) with scraped coconut, and a fruit
        • Fried rice packed with vegetables and egg, plus a side of fruit
        • Egg and vegetable sandwich with a banana
        Dinner
        • Rice with jackfruit (kos) curry, dhal and gotukola sambol
        • Rice with fish curry, a vegetable curry and a mallum
        • Kurakkan (finger millet) roti with a vegetable curry and a lunumiris on the side

        Simple snack ideas

        • Banana, papaya, guava, mango, pineapple or wood apple
        • Boiled green gram (mung), chickpeas or peanuts
        • Boiled or roasted corn, or sweet potato
        • Plain curd or yoghurt with fruit
        • King coconut water (thambili) on a hot day

        Sweets, fried snacks and fizzy drinks are fine sometimes. Enjoying food is part of being healthy.

        Food groups

        Staples

        Rice, red rice, bread, roti, string hoppers, cassava and sweet potato give your body energy for school and play.

        Vegetables and leaves

        Gotukola, murunga leaves, kankun, pumpkin, beans, brinjal and carrots add colour, fibre, vitamins and minerals. Try different colours through the week.

        Fruit

        Fruit is a tasty snack that also gives fibre and vitamins. Seasonal local fruit is often the cheapest and freshest.

        Protein foods

        Fish, sprats, eggs, dhal, mung, chickpeas, chicken and soya help your body grow and repair itself. Dhal and beans are affordable choices.

        Milk and alternatives

        Milk, curd and yoghurt provide calcium for strong bones. Small fish eaten with their bones also contain calcium.

        Fats and drinks

        Coconut, nuts and oily fish contain fats your body needs, in sensible amounts. Water is the everyday drink of choice.

        Mood history

          Breathing exercise

          Breathe in for 4 seconds, hold for 4, and breathe out for 6. Stop any time if you feel dizzy.

          Ready when you are

          Relaxation exercises

          5-4-3-2-1 grounding

          Look around and name 5 things you can see, 4 you can touch, 3 you can hear, 2 you can smell and 1 you can taste. Go slowly.

          Tense and release

          Squeeze your fists for 5 seconds, then let go. Do the same with your shoulders, then your legs. Notice how your body feels after each release.

          Neck and shoulder stretch

          Sit tall. Slowly tilt your ear toward your shoulder and hold for 15 seconds, then switch sides. Roll your shoulders back 5 times.

          Managing exam stress

          • Break revision into small tasks and tick them off.
          • Plan short sessions with breaks, rather than one long night.
          • Keep eating regular meals, drinking water and sleeping.
          • Talk to a teacher if a topic feels too big. Asking early helps.
          • Remember a result is one moment. It does not decide who you are.

          Healthy study breaks

          • Walk around the house or garden for 5 minutes
          • Drink a glass of water and eat a piece of fruit
          • Stretch, or do the breathing exercise above
          • Listen to one song, then go back to your desk
